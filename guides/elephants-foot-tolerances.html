How to Eliminate Elephant's Foot on Functional Snap-Fit Parts

Dimensional Calibration • Tolerance Benchmarks • Updated Fall 2026

Few things are more frustrating than printing a precision sliding lid or interlocking box, only to find the parts impossible to assemble. When you inspect the failure point, the culprit is almost always a flared, bulging ridge along the bottom 0.6 mm of the print: the classic Elephant's Foot.

This flare happens because the hot nozzle forces molten plastic downward against a heated bed to guarantee adhesion, squishing the bottom perimeters outward beyond their intended CAD profile. Here is how to kill this dimensional bulge without making your prints lift off the sheet mid-run.

1. The Slicer Solution: Elephant Foot Compensation

Modern slicers (Bambu Studio, OrcaSlicer, PrusaSlicer, and Cura) include a dedicated calculation to pull back initial perimeter paths. Instead of sanding finished parts with a hobby knife, let the g-code do the compensation:

  • PrusaSlicer / SuperSlicer: Set Elephant foot compensation to 0.15 mm - 0.20 mm.
  • OrcaSlicer / Bambu Studio: Adjust Initial layer elephant foot compensation to 0.15 mm.
  • Cura: Enable Initial Layer Horizontal Expansion and set it to a negative value: -0.15 mm.

2. Bed Temperature vs Glass Transition (Tg)

Keeping your heated bed right at the edge of the polymer's glass transition temperature creates perpetual slump under nozzle weight:

  • Standard PLA: Has a Tg around 55°C - 60°C. If your bed runs at 65°C throughout the entire print, the bottom layers stay rubbery and continue bulging outwards. Drop bed temperature to 55°C after layer 2.
  • PETG: Runs well at 70°C - 75°C on textured PEI. Avoid running 85°C unless you suffer severe draft cooling.

3. The 45-Degree Bottom Chamfer Trick

If you're generating custom CAD enclosures, the most foolproof mechanical fix is adding a subtle 0.5 mm × 45° chamfer along the base perimeter edge. Even if the first layer squishes outward slightly, the expansion simply fills the empty chamfer pocket without protruding beyond the outer functional wall.

4. Perfecting Z-Offset: Squish vs Over-Compression

Don't confuse bed leveling with excessive nozzle squish. A properly calibrated first layer should feature flat, fused ribbons without sharp plastic ridges pushing up between adjacent lines. If the nozzle plows trenches into the extruded plastic, raise your probe Z-offset by +0.02 mm to +0.04 mm.
